Why you get cold

A hammock doesn't lose heat upward. It loses it underneath.

You can be in a good sleeping bag and still wake up cold from below. The reason is simple, and it has nothing to do with how warm the bag is rated.

Down only insulates when it is lofted. Lie on it and your body weight flattens it to nothing. In a hammock the fabric pulls tight around you, so every bit of insulation underneath you is squashed flat exactly where you need it most.

The build

Box baffles. Not stitch‑through.

This is the detail that separates a real cold‑weather quilt from a cheap one, and it is the kind of thing you only find out about at 3am in November.

  • Box baffles, sewn as chambers A cheap quilt stitches the outer and inner shells straight together. Every seam is a line with no down in it, and a line with no down in it is a cold spot. Box baffles hold the two layers apart so the down fills right to the edge of every chamber.
  • 700 fill power, 90% premium duck down 1.76 lb of it. Higher fill power means more loft per gram, which means more warmth for less weight in your pack.
  • 400T nylon shell Light and quiet to the touch, tightly woven to keep the down where it belongs, and tough enough to live in a pack.
  • Full length, shoulder to foot 82″ × 50″. No three‑quarter gap at your calves, which is where a short quilt gives up.

Questions

The ones we actually get asked.

Will it fit the hammock I already have?

Yes. It fits every Hawk Nest model, V.1 through V.3, and it clips to the same carabiners your hammock already hangs from. There is nothing to re-rig and no extra hardware to buy.

Can I use this instead of a sleeping pad?

Yes, and that is the point of it. A pad has to be re-positioned every time you move and it squashes flat under your hips. The ThermoNest hangs outside the hammock so nothing presses on it. Plenty of people run both in deep cold, but for most nights the quilt alone does the job.

What does the comfort rating actually mean?

20°F to 0°F is the range most people sleep comfortably in with a closed end and a snug fit. The −13°F extreme figure is survival, not comfort. Sleep cold or sleep warm and your own number will sit either side of that, as with any quilt or bag.

What is a box baffle and why should I care?

A cheap quilt sews the outer and inner shells directly together. Every one of those seams is a line with no down in it, which is a cold stripe across your back. A box baffle uses a strip of fabric to hold the two shells apart, so the down fills the chamber right to the edge. More material, more labour, no cold stripes.

How do I store it?

Loose, never compressed. Down insulates by lofting and loft is what gets crushed. Keep it loosely folded in a large bin or box, or hanging in a closet. The compression sack is for the trail only. There is a plate on that above.
