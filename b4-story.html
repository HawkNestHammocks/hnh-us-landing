I spent three years
trying to fix
one bad night.

You know the night. You hiked all day, found the flattest ground there was — which wasn’t flat — and lay down on it. A root found your hip. The cold came up through the floor. At two in the morning you were still turning over, doing the arithmetic on how many hours were left.

Everyone who camps has had that night. Most of us decided it was the price of admission.

Chapter one

The ground was never the problem.

The problem was insisting on sleeping on it. Two trees will hold you off it entirely — no rocks, no roots, no puddle forming under your shoulder at 3am.

So I tried a hammock. And a hammock, on its own, is worse.

No bug net. No cover when it rains. And below 50°F, the air moving underneath turns it into a wind tunnel. It’s a nap, not a night’s sleep.

Which is how you end up carrying a hammock and a tarp and a net and an underquilt, and pitching four separate things in the dark.

Chapter two

So we sewed them together.

One shelter. The net is part of it. The rain fly goes over the top. The floor underneath is waterproof, and there’s a pocket in it that takes a sleeping pad up to 25 inches wide — which is the actual fix for the cold, because it puts insulation between you and the moving air.

The numbers
  • About 4 lbs on the trail
  • Packs to 5.5 x 5.5 x 16 in
  • Holds 500 lb
  • Pad pocket fits up to 25 in wide
  • Up in about four minutes, two trees, no poles or stakes
  • Navy, this run only
